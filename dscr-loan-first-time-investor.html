Can a First Time Investor Get a DSCR Loan? What Lenders Look At Instead

Yes, first time investors can get DSCR loans. Here is what lenders weigh instead of experience, where being new costs you, and how to build a strong first file.

Key Partners Funding6 min readFirst time rental investors

Yes, a first time investor can usually get a DSCR loan. Because the loan qualifies on the property's rent divided by its monthly payment rather than on your income, most DSCR programs do not require you to have owned a rental before. What changes for a first timer is the margin for error: some lenders want a stronger ratio, more cash in reserve, a lower loan to value, or proof that you own the home you live in, and pricing can be a little less favorable until you have a track record. The deal has to stand on its own, and you have to show you can carry it through a vacancy. Before anything else, run your property through our DSCR calculator to see where the ratio lands.

This guide covers what lenders look at when experience is missing, where being new tends to cost you, and how to decide whether a DSCR loan is the right first loan at all.

Why experience matters less on a DSCR loan than on a flip loan

On a fix and flip loan, the lender is betting on you to finish a renovation on budget and on time. Experience is a direct measure of that risk, which is why flip lenders tier their pricing and leverage by the number of completed projects. We cover that side in our guide to getting a hard money loan for your first flip.

A DSCR loan is a different bet. The property is already rentable, or will be at closing, and the loan is repaid from rent over thirty years. The lender's main question is whether the rent covers the payment with some room to spare. Your ability to manage a tenant matters, but the income test is the property, not your resume.

What lenders look at instead of experience

The ratio

The ratio is the core of the file. Rent divided by principal, interest, taxes, insurance and association dues. On our program, 1.25 and above is strong, 1.0 still qualifies, and below 1.0 does not. Some lenders ask first time investors for a higher minimum than they ask of experienced borrowers. Even where they do not, a ratio comfortably above 1.0 is the best thing a new investor can bring to the table. Our piece on what 1.25 versus 1.0 actually changes explains why.

Credit

Credit stands in for track record more than any other number. Our DSCR minimum is 620, but a higher score improves pricing and leverage. A first time investor with strong credit and a clean payment history is a much easier file than one with recent late payments, even if neither has owned a rental before.

Reserves

Reserves are cash left over after closing, measured in months of the property's payment. They answer the question every lender asks about a new landlord: what happens when the first tenant moves out or the HVAC fails in month three? Expect to show statements proving the money is in your accounts, and expect a lender to want more of it if this is your first property.

Down payment

Our DSCR program goes up to 80 percent loan to value. Whether a first time investor gets the top of that range depends on the rest of the file. A larger down payment lowers the payment, raises the ratio, and reduces the lender's exposure all at once, which is why it is often the simplest way to strengthen a first deal.

Home ownership

Some DSCR lenders require a first time investor to own their primary residence, on the reasoning that someone who has carried a mortgage understands a monthly payment and property expenses. Not every lender has this rule. If you rent your own home, ask before you apply rather than after.

Where being new tends to cost you

Being new rarely means a flat no. It more often shows up in the terms. Typical places it appears:

  • Pricing. A slightly higher rate or more points than the same file would get with a few rentals behind it.
  • Leverage. A lower maximum loan to value, meaning more cash down.
  • Property type. Some lenders limit first timers to single family homes before allowing two to four units.
  • Reserves. More months required than for a seasoned borrower.

None of these are permanent. After you have owned and managed a property for a year or two, you have the track record that unlocks better terms on the next one.

The LLC question

Our DSCR loans close in an LLC, not in your personal name. For a first time investor that means forming the entity, getting an EIN, writing an operating agreement and opening a business bank account before closing, not the week of. If you have not done this yet, our walkthrough of why DSCR lenders require an LLC and how to set one up lists the steps in order. Expect a lender to ask you to personally guarantee the loan even though the LLC is the borrower. Ask about this up front so it is not a surprise.

Is a DSCR loan the right first loan?

Not always. A DSCR loan is built for investors who want to qualify on the property and keep the loan off their personal income picture. For some first time buyers, a different path fits better.

When a DSCR loan makes sense

  • Your income is hard to document, comes from self employment, or is already stretched by other debt.
  • You plan to buy more than one rental and want a loan that scales without new income documents each time.
  • You want the property held in an LLC from day one.
  • The rent covers the payment with room to spare.

When a conventional loan might be cheaper

  • You have strong, easy to document W2 income and few existing mortgages.
  • You are fine owning the property in your personal name.
  • You are buying one rental and do not expect to buy more soon.

Conventional investment property loans often carry lower rates but require full income documentation and count the new payment against your debt to income ratio. Our comparison of hard money and conventional financing on an investment property covers the tradeoffs in more depth. If you plan to buy a property that needs work, the right first loan may be a rehab loan with a DSCR refinance later, rather than a DSCR loan on day one.

How to make a first DSCR file strong

  1. Pick a property that rents easily. A plain three bedroom house in a solid rental area is easier to underwrite and easier to lease than something unusual.
  2. Price the rent conservatively. Use leased comps, not asking rents, and assume the appraiser's rent schedule lands in the middle of the range.
  3. Get a real insurance quote early. Insurance is part of the payment, and a guess that comes in low can push the ratio under 1.0 at the last minute.
  4. Keep reserves separate and visible. Money that has sat in your account for a couple of months is easier to document than a recent deposit that needs explaining.
  5. Set up the LLC early. Entity paperwork is one of the most common delays on a first file.
  6. Clean up your credit before you apply. Pay down revolving balances and resolve anything in collections.

A first time investor with a property that cash flows, a clean credit file, real reserves and a ready LLC is not a hard file. Check the full list of DSCR loan requirements to see how your deal lines up, and send it over when you are ready for terms.
