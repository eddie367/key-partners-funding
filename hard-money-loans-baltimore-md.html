Hard Money Loans in Baltimore, MD

Fix and flip and rehab loans in Baltimore, built on what local flips actually look like: purchase prices, resale prices and hold times from county records.

Key Partners Funding5 min readInvestors flipping houses in Baltimore

A hard money loan, also called a fix and flip or rehab loan, finances the purchase and renovation of an investment property on the strength of the deal instead of your income. It is short term, interest only, and the rehab money is released in draws as the work gets done. Here is what flipping in Baltimore actually looks like right now, and what the financing costs on a typical deal.

What Baltimore flips look like, from county records

We tracked Baltimore City properties bought by an investor entity and resold by that same entity for more, between 45 days and 20 months later, using the county's recorded sales. Bank, government and iBuyer resales (companies like Opendoor) are left out. From 2025/09 through 2026/09 that came to 627 completed flips by 510 different investors.

Renovation flips are the classic fix and flip: buy a dated house, renovate it, sell it. The middle half of them bought between $95,000 and $182,000. The second group bought cheap, held longer and resold for more than double: full gut rehabs, additions, or a new house on the lot. Those need a bigger budget and a longer timeline, and a true rebuild runs on a construction loan rather than a rehab loan.

The typical renovation flip took about 6 months from purchase to sale, and 23% took 9 months or longer. The records cover about 12 months, so the longest projects do not show up yet and real hold times run somewhat longer. Budget your interest past your contractor's schedule, and pick a loan term with room to spare.

What the financing costs on a typical Baltimore flip

Take the typical renovation flip: a $145,000 purchase, an example rehab budget of $29,000 (20% of the price, for illustration), and a resale at the typical $269,000 after 189 days. With an example loan at 90% of total cost, 11% rate and 2 points, all assumed for illustration:

  • Loan amount: $156,600
  • Points at closing: $3,132
  • Interest over 6 months: about $8,925, figured on the full balance, so a loan that charges only on drawn rehab funds costs less
  • Selling costs at about 6% of the sale: $16,140

Out of the $125,000 gross spread, that leaves about $96,803 to cover the rehab and your profit. That is why the spread is not the profit, and why the hold time matters as much as the rate. Every extra month on this deal costs about $1,436 in interest. Work through monthly holding costs on a flip and points vs rate before you lock in a budget, and check how interest is charged with Dutch vs non Dutch interest.

Flip or keep it

If you would rather hold the house, a DSCR loan can refinance the rehab loan once it is leased. At Baltimore's median rent and home value, a DSCR loan works out to about 0.86, so rentals here usually need a lower purchase price or more down to work as an exit. See DSCR loans in Baltimore.
